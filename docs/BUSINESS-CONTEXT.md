# Better: where the business stands

For the conversations that are not about code: naming, branding,
positioning, pricing, launch, users. Read this before advising. It is
rewritten whenever `dev` is merged into `main`, so it describes what
people using the app have today.

Last updated: 14 November 2026, at release `1ecba5e`.

## The product in one paragraph

Google Calendar holds commitments with other people, at times other people
chose. Everything else — deep work, the gym, the call home — has no owner
and no time, which is most of why it does not happen. Better fits those
around the fixed things, says why it chose each hour and cites the research
behind it, records what actually happened, and reviews it. It reads the
calendar and never writes to it.

## Who it is for

People who want their life to have a structure, and who care more than
most about *when* is the best time to do each thing, for optimum
performance. Not exam candidates, not teams, not general time-trackers.

## What a person can do in the app today

- **Plan:** list what matters and get a time proposed for each, around the
  calendar, with the reason and its source. A usual hour learned from their
  own log outranks the textbook.
- **Your windows:** peak focus, the dip, the physical peak, wind-down —
  moved to when they wake — shown on Today with a reason for each.
- **Do and record:** tick off what was planned, confirm calendar events,
  or log anything in a tap.
- **Review:** a day and a week on three scores (Body, Mind, Heart), plus
  how much focused work, exercise and time with people fell inside their
  windows — stated as time, never as a fourth score.
- **Reminders:** log your morning at 12, afternoon at 6, evening at 9, in
  their own timezone, skipped when already logged.
- **The science:** a library of the studies, cited from wherever they are
  used.
- **Their data:** export everything, or delete the account.
- **Install:** a web app added to the phone's Home Screen. Not in any app
  store.

## Where it stands

- **Live** at time-logger-nine.vercel.app. Used by the founder and a few
  friends. Free.
- **Name:** "Better" is a placeholder. No name, no domain yet. This is the
  next decision and it blocks the three items below.
- **Homepage:** none. Needs the name and domain.
- **Google sign-in:** works for anyone, but the app is unverified, so new
  people see Google's warning once and there is a lifetime cap of 100
  users. Verification needs a homepage and privacy policy on an owned
  domain, and a short video of the sign-in.
- **Sign-in options:** Google only. Moving to a service that adds Apple
  and email sign-in is planned and parked; it also wants an owned domain.
- **Payments:** none built, on purpose.

## Decisions already made

- **Don't charge until retention holds.** The number: of people who finish
  onboarding, how many complete the loop — plan, do, review — on four or
  more days in their second week. Under a third, fix the product first.
- **Pricing, when it comes:** about $59–69 a year or $7–8 a month, with a
  lifetime offer of about $120 for early users, a 14-day trial, and no ads
  or data sales.
- **Home Screen, not app stores,** for now.
- **Never overclaim the science.** The findings are averages across
  people. Saying how strong each one is, is the brand.
- **No streaks, no guilt, no "we miss you".** Three scores and facts.
- **Launch target:** the first week of January, when people who want
  structure go looking for it.
- **Sequence:** ten to twenty people like the audience first; then pages
  answering "the best time to…" questions; then communities, with
  something to show; no paid acquisition until people are paying.

## Open questions

- The name, and the domain that goes with it.
- Whether the first ten to twenty people are completing the loop. Nobody
  is measuring it yet.
- Whether to offer Apple sign-in, which needs a paid developer account.

## Voice

Plain, calm, specific. Short sentences that say what a thing does. No
hype, no emoji, no exclamation marks. Promise in use: "The right hour for
everything", or shorter, "Know when".

## Competitors, briefly

RISE (about $70 a year) and Lifestack (about $7 a month or $50 a year)
lead on sleep and wearable data. Chronotype planners and auto-schedulers
sit either side. Better's ground is the whole day, the evidence, and the
loop — not wearables. Prices were checked in November 2026; check again
before quoting them.
